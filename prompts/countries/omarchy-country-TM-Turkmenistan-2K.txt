A cinematic 16:9 photorealistic digital painting of a Turkmenistan desert landscape at golden hour: the vast red-ochre sand of the Karakum Desert rolling in long smooth dunes toward the white-marble skyline of Ashgabat on the horizon. Wide, low camera placed on the desert floor, horizon set slightly below the middle of the frame so the sky fills the upper 55% of the image.

FOREGROUND (bottom third): a modern two-lane asphalt highway enters from the bottom-center-right of the frame, very close to the camera, its dark blue-grey tarmac occupying roughly the lower-right quarter of the picture with a bright double yellow centre line and a pale worn shoulder line. The road sweeps to the left, then curves back to the right and narrows into a thin serpentine ribbon as it climbs across the dunes toward the distant city, its S-curve reading clearly through the midground. Along both shoulders, low sandy embankments of compacted ochre earth with scattered clumps of dry desert grass, saxaul scrub and thin silver-green tussocks, each clump only knee-high. A small white-and-red concrete roadside marker post stands on the left verge about one third from the bottom edge, barely 1 metre tall, giving scale. Wind-carved sand ridges and faint tyre-blown dust drift across the tarmac edge.

MIDGROUND (left side): a slightly raised sandy ridge on the left, about one third up from the bottom edge, carries a single wild Turkmen kulan (Asiatic wild ass, Equus hemionus kulan) standing in profile, facing right toward the road and the city. Realistic proportions: the animal is roughly 1.2 metres at the shoulder, small in the frame — no taller than a few percent of the image height, clearly smaller than the roadside dunes behind it and tiny compared to the distant buildings. Sandy-fawn coat, pale creamy belly and muzzle, dark erect mane and dark dorsal stripe, short tufted black-tipped tail, slender legs. It casts a long thin shadow to the left across the rippled sand. No saddle, no rider, no herd — one lone animal.

BACKGROUND: successive waves of soft red-gold dunes recede toward the centre, becoming hazier and cooler with distance; a larger dune shoulder rises on the right edge of the frame, cut by the road cutting, and a low dune range closes the far left. On the horizon, centred and slightly left-of-centre, the skyline of Ashgabat: gleaming white marble buildings with colonnades and classical porticos, several white apartment and office towers, and two large gilded golden domes — one smaller on the left, one large brilliant gold dome on a white-columned palace just right of centre. Between them rises a slender white-and-gold monument tower with a tapered tripod-like shaft and a small golden crown at the top (an Ashgabat neutrality-style monument column), the tallest element of the skyline yet still modest — only about one eighth of the image height, dwarfed by the width of the desert. A thin band of dark green trees and irrigated gardens marks the city edge where the sand meets the buildings; faint low city blocks fade into golden haze on the far right.

LIGHT AND COLOUR: late afternoon sun low behind the city, warm backlight flaring softly on the horizon, sand lit in amber, terracotta, apricot and rose tones with long violet-blue shadows in the dune hollows. Sky graduated from deep clean blue at the top-left to warm cream and peach near the horizon, streaked with layered altocumulus clouds lit orange and pink on their undersides, drifting from upper left to upper right. Dry, clear, silent desert atmosphere with a faint heat haze over the city.

Style: photorealistic cinematic digital painting, matte-painting quality, ultra wide vista, 16:9 aspect ratio, highly detailed, natural realistic scale for animal and architecture, soft volumetric golden-hour light, subtle film grain, high dynamic range, wallpaper composition with clean uncluttered foreground.
