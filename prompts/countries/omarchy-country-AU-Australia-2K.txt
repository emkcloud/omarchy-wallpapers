A cinematic 16:9 photorealistic digital painting of the Australian Red Centre outback at golden hour, seen from a high red-sandstone escarpment looking out over a vast desert valley. The scene is unmistakably Australia: burnt-orange iron-rich rock, spinifex-dotted arid plains, a monolithic sandstone inselberg on the horizon, and a kangaroo in the foreground.

FOREGROUND (bottom third, running left to right): weathered red sandstone ledges and boulders forming a natural viewing platform. On the left and lower-left, a large layered cliff shoulder of stratified orange-red rock with deep vertical fissures, blocky fractured slabs and rounded boulders, occupying roughly the left quarter of the frame and dropping away into a shadowed canyon. On the right and lower-right, a broad flat sandstone shelf of the same red rock, cracked and sun-scoured, tilting gently toward the viewer. Sparse tufts of pale golden spinifex grass and small dry desert shrubs grow from cracks in the rock at the bottom edge and lower-left. A small isolated sandstone pinnacle/hoodoo stands in the left-center middle distance, about the height of two people.

THE ANIMAL: a single adult red kangaroo (Macropus rufus) stands upright in profile on the sandstone shelf on the right side of the frame, at roughly 65-70% of the image width and sitting in the lower-right quadrant. It rests on its hind legs and thick tapered tail in the classic tripod stance, head turned toward the left to look out across the valley, small ears erect, forelimbs held loosely at the chest. Its fur is warm reddish-brown with a paler cream chest and muzzle, lit from behind-right by the low sun so its edges glow. STRICTLY REALISTIC SCALE: the kangaroo is about 1.5 metres tall — clearly a mid-size mammal, small against the immense landscape, roughly one fifth of the image height, dwarfed by the cliffs and utterly tiny compared to the distant monolith. It must never approach the size of a hill, a rock formation, or a building. It casts a long soft shadow to the left across the rock.

MIDGROUND: below and beyond the foreground cliffs, a wide open desert valley of ochre, rust and dusty olive tones spreads across the center of the frame. A winding watercourse — a pale turquoise-blue desert river — enters from the lower-center-right, curves in a long lazy S back through the middle of the valley and narrows as it recedes toward the monolith, functioning as the leading line of the composition in place of a road. Its banks are edged with narrow strips of green riverine scrub, small mulga and desert oak trees, and scattered dark bushes that thin out into the plain. Low red-rock mesas, terraces and eroded ridges flank both sides of the valley at mid-distance, no more than a few tens of metres tall.

BACKGROUND / LANDMARK: dominating the center-background, slightly left of the exact center and sitting on the horizon line, rises a huge isolated sandstone monolith in the form of Uluru / Ayers Rock — a long, rounded, whale-backed dome of deep orange-red rock with distinctive vertical erosion channels and ribbed flutes carved down its steep flanks, a gently domed summit and a broad flat base skirted by low green scrub. It is enormous but correctly proportioned: about 350 metres high and several kilometres long, spanning roughly one third of the image width and glowing intense fiery red-orange in the low sun, its far side falling into cool violet shadow. Beyond it, faint blue-violet ranges and low flat-topped mesas fade in atmospheric haze along the horizon on both the far left and far right.

Fine dust haze softens the far distance. Palette: burnt orange, terracotta, iron red, ochre and gold against cobalt and cerulean blue, with turquoise river accents and violet distant shadows.
