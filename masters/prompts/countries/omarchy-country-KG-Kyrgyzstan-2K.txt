A cinematic 16:9 photorealistic digital painting of a high-altitude summer landscape in Kyrgyzstan, evoking the alpine pastures (jailoo) around Song-Kul and the Tian Shan range at golden hour. The composition is a wide, deep panorama built around a winding asphalt road, a nomadic yurt camp, an alpine lake and a wild mountain sheep.

FOREGROUND (bottom third): a two-lane asphalt road enters from the bottom-left corner, occupying roughly the lower-right quarter of the frame, and sweeps in a long S-curve toward the middle-right before disappearing behind a grassy rise near the lake. The asphalt is dark blue-grey with fine gravel texture, a broken double yellow centre line and a continuous white edge line on the right side; the shoulder is loose gravel and dry grass. On the left, above the road embankment, a low grassy hillside covered in sunlit golden-green summer grass, small scattered granite boulders and tufts of dry stalks catching the low light. Standing on this left grassy verge, in profile facing right toward the valley, a single adult wild ram — a Marco Polo argali / Central Asian mountain sheep with a stocky brown-grey body, pale cream muzzle and rump, thick shaggy neck and enormous spiralling ridged horns curling in a full circle beside its head. Its scale is strictly realistic: about 1.2 m at the shoulder, roughly the height of the road's guard shoulder, occupying only about 10-12% of the frame's height and clearly much smaller than the distant yurts and hills; it is warmly rimlit by the low sun, casting a long soft shadow to the left across the grass.

MIDGROUND (centre and right, middle band of the frame): rolling emerald-and-gold grass hills fold down toward a long, narrow alpine lake that occupies the centre-right of the middle distance, its still surface a cool steel-blue reflecting the sky, with a pale gravel shoreline. On the right bank, on a gentle green terrace above the lake, a small nomadic camp of seven to nine traditional Kyrgyz yurts (boz üy): low white felt domes with wooden door frames painted with red and orange tribal ornament, tiny compared with the hills — each yurt no larger than a fingertip in scale, clearly reading as small round tents about 5 m wide. Beside and behind them, a scattered string of tiny grazing horses (bay, chestnut and grey), each far smaller than the yurts' width, dotted along the slope. A few sparse conifers and small dark spruce clusters dot the left and centre-left slopes, tiny against the hills to convey vast scale.

BACKGROUND (upper half): a massive Tian Shan mountain range fills the horizon — sharp pyramidal granite peaks with snow caps and glaciers, warm ochre and rose-lit ridges facing the sun, deep blue-violet shadowed faces, receding in atmospheric haze into pale golden layers on the right. The tallest central peak rises just left of centre and dominates the skyline. Above, a deep cobalt-blue sky in the upper left grading to warm amber and pale gold toward the right horizon, streaked with wispy cirrus clouds lit peach and cream, brushed in long diagonal strokes.

LIGHTING AND MOOD: late-afternoon golden-hour sun low on the right, casting warm raking light across the grass and mountain faces, long soft shadows, gentle aerial perspective, crisp clean high-altitude air, serene and expansive nomadic solitude.
