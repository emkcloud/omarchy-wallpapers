A cinematic 16:9 photorealistic digital painting of the highlands of Lesotho, the "Kingdom in the Sky", seen from a grassy ridge at golden hour, looking down into a wide montane valley of the Maloti-Drakensberg. The whole scene is an open, treeless alpine grassland plateau cut by deep green valleys, unmistakably Basotho highland country.

Foreground, lower-left and along the bottom edge: a steep tussock-grass slope in warm ochre and olive green, scattered with weathered grey-brown sandstone and dolerite boulders of knee to waist height, some larger slabs in the lower-right corner. Blades of dry golden grass catch rim light. On this slope, positioned in the left third of the frame and standing in profile facing right, a single adult common eland (Taurotragus oryx) — tawny-grey coat, faint pale flank stripes, heavy dewlap, straight spiralled horns, dark tail tuft, black knee patches. Realistic proportions: the animal is about 1.6 m at the shoulder, roughly one third the height of the frame at this near distance, clearly smaller than the huts in the valley when scale is compared, never oversized.

Left edge, upper-middle: the ridge continues upward into a flat-topped grassy shoulder capped with a low band of eroded reddish sandstone cliffs and rock outcrops, framing the composition like a natural repoussoir.

Midground, centre-right: the valley floor drops away in smooth, rounded, deeply green-and-gold grassy hills with terraced contour lines and small dark bush clumps in the folds. A narrow two-lane tarred mountain road, pale grey with a faint centre line, enters from the right-centre foreground and snakes away in long S-bends — first curving left, then right — climbing gently up the valley and shrinking to a thread near the centre horizon. It occupies roughly the central third of the image and is modest in width, in scale with the vast landscape.

Perched on a small green spur just above the second bend of the road, right of centre and clearly in the middle distance: a small Basotho village of six to eight traditional rondavels — round mud-and-stone huts with ochre walls and conical thatched roofs — each hut small in the frame (about the size of a fingernail at this distance), with low stone kraal walls, a footpath and a few cattle dots nearby. The huts are realistically proportioned: an adult person would reach roughly to the eaves.

Background, centre-left: a dramatic ridge of jagged basalt peaks and buttresses of the Maloti / Drakensberg escarpment, blue-grey and layered, with sheer cliff faces and pinnacles catching warm side light on their western flanks. Behind and to the right, successive receding ranges fade into pale blue and lilac atmospheric haze, low mist lying in the far valleys, with soft rolling foothills closing the right side of the frame.

Lighting: late-afternoon golden hour, the sun low and just out of frame to the right, casting long warm raking light across the grass slopes and the eland, deep cool blue-violet shadows in the valley folds, glowing rim light on the animal's back and horns. Sky fills the upper half: deep cobalt blue at the top-left, huge sunlit cumulus clouds with brilliant white and cream tops piled over the mountains in the upper centre-left, and warm apricot, amber and pink cirrus streaks over the low sun on the right horizon.

Colour palette: golden amber, warm ochre, sap and emerald green, russet brown rock, cobalt and cerulean sky, lilac-blue distant peaks. Atmosphere: vast, serene, high-altitude clarity with a faint haze, epic and peaceful.

Style: photorealistic cinematic digital matte painting, wide-angle vista, 16:9 aspect ratio, highly detailed, crisp textures on grass, rock, thatch and animal fur, natural volumetric light, no text, no people in the foreground.
