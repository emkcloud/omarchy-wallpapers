A cinematic 16:9 photorealistic digital painting of a coastal mangrove estuary in Guinea-Bissau, seen from a slightly elevated wide-angle viewpoint at golden hour, evoking the Bijagos Archipelago and the tidal creeks of the Rio Geba delta. The scene is a low, flat tropical coastline: no mountains, only sand dunes, tidal flats, mangrove islets, palm groves and the open Atlantic on the far horizon.

FOREGROUND (bottom third): a broad, pale sandy dirt track enters from the bottom-center of the frame and winds in a soft S-curve toward the right and into the middle distance, its surface a warm cream-beige braided with faint tyre ruts and drifted sand. Low scrub, tufts of coarse dune grass and small green bushes line both edges of the track. In the bottom-left corner, dense dark-green coastal shrubs and a low windswept mangrove-edge tree with thin twisting branches frame the image; in the bottom-right corner, the fronds of a young palm and broad-leafed tropical foliage lean into the frame as a dark silhouetted repoussoir.

MIDGROUND LEFT AND CENTER: a wide, glassy tidal channel of shallow brackish water reflecting the peach and pale-blue sky. Along the left bank sprawls a thick mangrove forest of Rhizophora with exposed reddish stilt roots arching out of the mud and rounded olive-green canopies, occupying roughly the left third of the frame and standing only about 4-6 meters tall relative to the huge sky. Small mangrove islets and sandbars dot the channel as it snakes from the left-center back toward the horizon.

THE ANIMAL: a single adult common hippopotamus (the famous saltwater hippos of the Orango Islands) rests half-submerged in the channel at center-left, at about one third of the image height from the bottom. Only its broad back, rounded rump, ears and blunt muzzle break the surface, catching warm rim light; gentle ripples spread around it. Realistic scale: the hippo reads as roughly 3.5 meters long and clearly much smaller than the palm trees and the huts — never larger than a small boat, never oversized against the landscape.

MIDGROUND RIGHT — ARCHITECTURE: a small traditional Bijago-style tabanca village sits on the sandy rise on the right side, occupying about one sixth of the frame width. Two or three circular mud-and-timber huts with tall conical thatched palm-leaf roofs, ochre-brown walls, a dark low doorway, a leaning wooden drying rack and a few gourds and fishing poles beside them. The huts are modest single-storey structures roughly 4 meters tall, correctly larger than the hippo and dwarfed by the surrounding palms. A slender wooden dugout canoe (pirogue) lies beached on the sand just behind and left of the village, near the water's edge.

VEGETATION: a scattered grove of tall coconut and oil palms rises across the center-right, their crowns at about 12-18 meters, silhouetted and rim-lit by the low sun; more palms cluster around the village on the far right.

BACKGROUND: beyond the estuary, low pale sand dunes with sparse green vegetation stretch across the middle-left horizon; further back, a thin bright band of the Atlantic Ocean with soft breaking surf runs across the full width of the frame, blending into a warm haze. No hills or mountains — the land stays flat and oceanic.

LIGHTING AND ATMOSPHERE: late golden-hour sunset, the sun low and just out of frame on the right, casting long warm light across the sand and setting the water and wet mud aglow. Big dramatic tropical sky filling the upper half: gradient from soft blue at the top-left through apricot, salmon and pale gold near the horizon on the right, with streaks of thin cirrus and wispy altocumulus catching pink and orange highlights. Warm humid coastal haze, gentle atmospheric perspective, calm and serene mood.
